A Central Question The 2026 Agenda Beyond "The Future" Systemic Influence Carrying Forward

Mohamed Naguib, Founder and Chairperson of Balanced Egypt, represented the organization at the 2026 ECOSOC Youth Forum in New York. The experience brought a central question for youth-led sustainable development into focus: how can participation in global dialogue translate into sustained institutional influence and practical action?

From 14 to 16 April 2026, Mohamed Naguib represented Balanced Egypt at the United Nations Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC) Youth Forum at United Nations Headquarters in New York. Held under the theme “Innovate, Unite and Transform: Youth Shaping the Road to 2030,” the forum brought together young people, Member States, United Nations entities and other stakeholders to consider how youth-led solutions and international cooperation can accelerate progress towards the Sustainable Development Goals.

The 2026 Agenda & Focus

Organized by the UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs, the annual forum provides a platform for young people to contribute perspectives and implementation experience to wider development discussions. The 2026 edition focused on:

SDG 06 & 07

Water & Clean Energy

Addressing clean water, sanitation, and affordable, clean energy transitions for sustainable communities.

SDG 09 & 11

Infrastructure & Cities

Focusing on industry, innovation, resilient infrastructure, and sustainable cities and communities.

The agenda also highlighted SDG 17 (Partnerships for the Goals). These were the goals selected for in-depth review at the 2026 High-level Political Forum on Sustainable Development.

Beyond "The Future"

Beyond the thematic agenda, the forum raised a question about the institutional conditions under which youth participation becomes consequential. In her opening address, Annalena Baerbock, President of the 80th session of the United Nations General Assembly, challenged the familiar description of young people as “the future,” warning that the phrase can become “a way of postponing responsibility.” Her argument was that young people are already affected by, and actively responding to, the challenges that development policy seeks to address.

In his public reflection following the forum, Naguib connected this observation to Balanced Egypt’s own experience. The organization had already begun developing campaigns, workshops and research around food waste, responding to a problem that exists in communities today rather than waiting for a future invitation to act. His reflection placed the significance of participation in a particular context: youth-led initiatives are not simply preparing to contribute to sustainable development—they are already undertaking work within it.

From Participation to Systemic Influence

For Balanced Egypt, that work begins with food waste and its social, environmental and economic consequences. Through community engagement, educational activities and research, the organization seeks to connect greater awareness with practical approaches to more sustainable consumption. The relevance of this work extends beyond individual behaviour; the ways communities address food waste are also shaped by access to information, collective practices, institutional support and the conditions that make more sustainable choices possible.

"Participation must translate into real, systemic influence, with youth engagement sustained and connected to policy outcomes."

Food-waste prevention was not identified as a central forum-wide theme, and Mohamed’s participation should not be confused with a formal presentation on the subject. The more substantive connection lies in the wider development question shared by youth-led local initiatives and multilateral institutions: how are broad commitments translated into practice, and what enables practical solutions to be sustained beyond individual projects?

The forum’s closing remarks made the institutional dimension explicit. Participation must translate into real, systemic influence, with youth engagement sustained and connected to policy outcomes. The forum’s recommendations were intended to inform the High-level Political Forum and other intergovernmental processes, including the 2027 SDG Summit. These pathways illustrate how youth perspectives can enter wider policy discussions, while leaving the responsibility for decisions and implementation with the institutions and actors involved.

Carrying the Questions Forward

For youth-led organizations, this distinction matters. Access to international dialogue can create opportunities to learn, exchange perspectives and understand how development priorities are negotiated and reviewed. But attendance alone does not establish influence, just as a local initiative’s existence does not, by itself, demonstrate its wider impact.

For Balanced Egypt, engagement with the 2026 ECOSOC Youth Forum brings this relationship into sharper focus. Its significance lies not simply in where the organization was represented, but in the question that representation raises for its ongoing work: how can youth-led action remain connected to the wider systems that shape sustainable development?

Ultimately, the value of youth participation cannot be measured by presence alone. It depends on whether young people’s contributions are taken seriously, whether the institutions around them create pathways for sustained engagement, and whether the work undertaken in communities can contribute to the broader effort to turn development commitments into practical progress.
