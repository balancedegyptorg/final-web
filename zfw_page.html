Overview

In February 2025, Balanced Egypt hosted Zero Food Waste at the Academy of Scientific Research and Technology, in collaboration with the Children's University, Global Shapers Alexandria, House Garden, and Duck Donuts. Designed as an educational experience spanning multiple generations, the event brought children, teenagers, and adults together through parallel sessions running at the same time, each one built around what mattered most to that stage of life.

The Challenge

Food waste education in Egypt is often limited to awareness campaigns that never measure whether anyone actually learned something. It also tends to stay disconnected from practical, household level solutions, and rarely sits within a scientific or institutional framework that gives it lasting credibility.

Our Approach

We built three distinct educational tracks, one each for children, teenagers, and adults, with curricula tailored to what was relevant at each stage of life: awareness and foundational understanding for children, responsibility and behavioral ownership for teenagers, and community level accountability for adults. Every track included assessments before and after the sessions so we could measure real learning, not just attendance.

The founder of House Garden led a session on home agriculture as a practical way to reduce food waste and improve household food systems. The day closed with an expert panel moderated by Mohamed Naguib, Founder of Balanced Egypt, featuring Dr. Fayrouz Ashour, Assistant Professor of Ecology at AUC, Dr. Hossam Awad, Lecturer in Ecology at Cairo University, and Dr. Aya AlKhouly, Professor at AUC and a member of the Children's University Board.

What We Measured

Every track showed a measurable increase in awareness, with the strongest shift among children and meaningful gains among both adults and teenagers.

Looking Ahead

Zero Food Waste showed us that a single event can move the needle across three very different age groups at once, as long as the content actually speaks to where people are in life. We're using that model as a blueprint for future multi generational programming, and continuing to build the kind of partnerships, from universities to local businesses, that make events like this possible.
