Youth Research Initiative

Beyond The Bin

Youth Research into Green Innovation, Sustainability & Behavioural Change

Location

Cairo University

Dates

13 to 15 July 2026

Partners

Cairo University & Greenish (EU Funded)

01. What Happened 02. How It Worked 03. The Results 04. Looking Ahead

What Happened

Fifty young Egyptians was the plan. Seventy six showed up. Over three days in Cairo, a gender balanced group of students aged 13 to 17 traded passive awareness of environmental issues for something sturdier: the tools to research them, argue about them, and propose real solutions to them. The programme was hosted at Cairo University, delivered in partnership with Greenish, and funded by the European Union.

Most Egyptian teenagers already know sustainability matters. Few have ever been handed the vocabulary, tools, or global context to engage with it properly, and fewer still have been treated as capable of doing the kind of applied research usually reserved for university students. Beyond The Bin was built to close exactly that gap, grounded in Egypt's own food waste challenges rather than an imported curriculum, and designed for young people, not university students.

How It Worked

01

Food Waste & Sustainability

Modules covering food waste systems, Egypt's environmental context, consumer behaviour, and the science behind why habits actually change.

02

Research & Project Development

Modules on formulating research questions, collecting evidence, designing solutions, and presenting findings, taught in parallel rather than after.

The two tracks were never sequential. A session on green innovation fed straight into a research exercise using local environmental data. A discussion of behavioural change connected directly to how behavioural research itself gets designed. By the third day, participants weren't just learning about sustainability, they were practising thinking about it the way researchers do.

Working in teams of five to seven, each group chose a food waste challenge on day one and carried it through every session that followed. By the close of day three, every team had produced a short written research brief and delivered a presentation to their peers and facilitators, real research, with their names on it, that they can point to in future academic or exchange applications.

The programme closed with a session most participants had never had access to before: a direct conversation with a representative from a European institution, covering sustainability from a European lens, what young people are already achieving in this space, and the international exchange and research opportunities open to them next. It was less a farewell than a reframe, positioning every participant as a future contributor to a conversation that extends well beyond Egypt.

The Results

0%

Rise in Food Waste Awareness

Rise in Research Literacy

Those numbers describe something more than a successful workshop. An 87.4 percent rise in food waste awareness means an idea that used to be abstract is now something participants can explain, argue for, and act on. A 92 percent rise in research literacy means they didn't just learn about a problem, they learned how to investigate one, a skill that outlasts any single topic.

Looking Ahead

Every team's research brief will be published on Balanced Egypt's and Greenish's platforms, giving each participant a citable, public output before they've even finished secondary school. The alumni network seeded over these three days is designed to outlast the programme itself, connecting participants to future opportunities, and eventually turning some of them into co-facilitators for the next edition.

Beyond The Bin was built to be replicable. Cairo was only the first city.
