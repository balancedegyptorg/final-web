Why Balanced Egypt

A more considered approach to food waste starts with understanding it.

A youth-led Egyptian initiative focused on the social, environmental, and economic dimensions of food waste.

Food waste is more than what gets thrown away. It raises questions about how food is valued, used, and understood, and how everyday decisions connect to wider sustainability challenges.

The Issue

Food waste is a practical entry point into sustainability.

A focused issue can open a wider conversation about resources, responsibility, and everyday decisions.

Food waste connects individual and institutional practices with broader questions about resource use, responsible consumption, and the social and economic value of food.

For educational institutions and organizations working on sustainability, it offers a tangible subject through which these connections can be explored, making room for questions people can examine in familiar settings.

Our Approach

Education, awareness, and research, each with a distinct role.

Different approaches help people engage with food waste from different starting points.

Educational Engagement

Food-waste education creates an opportunity to examine the issue, explore its wider implications, and discuss how it relates to everyday practices.

For an educational institution or community partner, an activity begins with a clear question: what should participants understand about food waste, and what would a useful learning experience look like in their setting?

Institutional Relevance: Designed to integrate smoothly into curricula, workshops, and campus sustainability seminars.

Public Awareness

Public awareness helps bring attention to the social, environmental, and economic dimensions of food waste.

Campaigns provide a starting point for accessible communication, encourage discussion, and help make a defined issue more visible. Their reach should be distinguished from evidence of learning.

Institutional Relevance: Fosters broader community dialogues and highlights sustainability priorities across student networks.

Research and Inquiry

Research can help clarify what is known, identify questions that require further investigation, and strengthen the basis on which awareness initiatives are developed.

Any research findings are presented in line with methods used, people or settings studied, and the limits of available evidence.

Institutional Relevance: Establishes an evidence-informed foundation for academic inquiry and project scoping.

Evidence & Learning

Distinguishing activity from impact.

We evaluate our work across four distinct tiers to maintain institutional honesty.

Activity Delivery

What took place? This includes workshops held, materials produced, or research sessions completed. It confirms operational execution but does not measure behavioural change.

Participant Reach

Who attended or engaged? Counting participants or partner institutions provides a measure of visibility and distribution, distinct from educational depth.

Demonstrated Learning

What did participants take away? Assessing actual shifts in awareness or expressed attitudes requires structured feedback, surveys, or reflective evaluations.

Longer-Term Change

Did practices or policies shift over time? Lasting transformation in institutional habits or resource management requires longitudinal study beyond initial activities.
