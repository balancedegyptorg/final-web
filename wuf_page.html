Overview

In October 2024, Balanced Egypt was selected as a Community Partner for the World Urban Forum for Children, chosen from more than 2,500 NGOs. The forum was hosted at the American University in Cairo, in collaboration with the AUC School of Sciences and Engineering, the Academy of Scientific Research and Technology, the Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research, and several national and international NGOs. Within this global gathering on urban sustainability, we designed and led interactive workshops exploring how food waste connects to the cities children grow up in.

The Challenge

Conversations about food waste rarely make it into urban planning or child friendly city design, even though children are directly affected by food systems, waste infrastructure, and environmental change happening around them. On top of that, environmental education for children often lacks any way to measure whether it actually worked, and youth led organizations are still underrepresented in institutional urban sustainability platforms like this one.

Our Approach

We translated food waste and urban sustainability concepts into frameworks children could actually relate to, then built interactive, activity based sessions around them. Content was tailored by age group, and every session included a pre and post assessment so we could measure what children learned, not just how much fun they had. The goal was to connect a global conversation about urban development to something children could understand and act on locally.

What We Measured

Using structured pre and post assessments, we tracked how much children's understanding shifted over the course of the workshops. The results showed that youth led, research informed education can genuinely bridge a global sustainability agenda with child centered learning.

Looking Ahead

WUF12 confirmed something we already believed: children understand sustainability faster than adults expect, when it's explained in terms they can see around them. We're carrying that lesson into future partnerships with universities, ministries, and international forums, and continuing to make the case that youth led organizations belong in these conversations from the start.
